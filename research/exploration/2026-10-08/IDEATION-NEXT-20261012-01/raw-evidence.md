## N05 — Matched role comparison on one fixed task

- **Lens:** Decision-linked evidence; whether a role split merits further use.
- **Proposal:** Compare one fixed, bounded fictional task under a single-agent condition and a role-separated condition, holding the task, source packet, acceptance rubric, time budget, and configured model constant where available. Randomize condition order and have a reviewer unaware of condition labels assess the outputs. Make a small feasibility pilot, not a claim about general performance.
- **Evidence hook:** The accepted review says the 06 presentation conditions used four workers, one per condition, confounding worker with condition; the 05/06 records also share clustered cells/history. A matched design addresses that specific comparison gap. It does not establish backend identity or eliminate carryover/session effects.
- **Observable test and decision:** In an approximately 60-minute future session, use one preselected task with two short runs per condition only if scheduling permits, record deviations and elapsed/active time separately, and apply a frozen rubric for supported answers, material errors, and completion. Stop if the setup cannot keep task and inputs fixed. The result can decide whether a larger matched comparison is worth commissioning or whether to retain the simpler arrangement; a tiny pilot cannot demonstrate superiority or reliability.
- **Prerequisites / unknowns:** Owner must name the decision and approve a task/rubric; session availability, model/backend stability, and reviewer time are unknown. If only one condition can be run, report feasibility alone. No new 256-case expansion is assumed.
- **Tradeoff:** Matching reduces one confound but consumes scarce session/reviewer time and may introduce learning or order effects. One task is not representative.

## N06 — Blinded claim-to-source adjudication of boundary cases

- **Lens:** Decision-linked evidence; whether current answer evidence is strong enough for reuse or merits a narrow support-checking tool.
- **Proposal:** Independently adjudicate a small, predeclared set of answer claims against the exact source scope/date and served-byte records, including the known wrong-scope false negative and sequence confusion, a supported positive, and a genuinely unresolved example. Hide presentation labels during adjudication. Keep “bytes served,” “answer supported,” and “model read/comprehended” as separate outcomes.
- **Evidence hook:** The accepted review reports a wrong-scope answer marked unresolved despite a false-negative explanation and a wrong event sequence in 06; it also stresses that service receipts do not prove model reading or arbitrary prose correctness. This probe targets claim support only. It cannot establish hidden reading, script absence, or broad factual accuracy.
- **Observable test and decision:** In about 60 minutes, freeze up to six claim/source pairs before review; a separate assessor records the relevant source passage, scope/date admissibility, and supported / unsupported / unresolved outcome with a short reason. Compare the adjudication with the preserved structured labels and explanations. If disagreement is material even on this deliberately small boundary set, decide whether a narrow claim-support workflow is useful; otherwise keep the evidence corpus and do not infer that a checker is needed. The sample is diagnostic, not a rate estimate.
- **Prerequisites / unknowns:** Exact source and receipt bytes must be available and linkable to each selected claim; the value review identifies records but this proposed adjudication is unrun. A reviewer must not infer actual model access from a matching citation alone.
- **Tradeoff:** Known error cases make the probe interpretable but make it unsuitable for estimating prevalence or transfer. Manual adjudication is slower than a checker and does not test arbitrary prose.

## N07 — Reproducibility check for classification evidence

- **Lens:** Decision-linked evidence; whether script-based classifications can be audited independently before they support reuse claims.
- **Proposal:** For a future small run, freeze the raw inputs, executable classification script and version, invocation, expected outputs, and a separately written manual rubric before results are inspected. Ask a reviewer to reproduce a preselected small sample from the frozen inputs and compare it with the reported labels. Treat missing historical scripts as unavailable evidence; do not fabricate a replay of 05.
- **Evidence hook:** The accepted review says 05 executors reported classification scripts but the snippets were temporary and unexported, so exact classifier reproduction is unavailable; raw/receipt/grading reproduction remains possible. The proposal tests reproducibility of future classification, not whether a past worker secretly used a script.
- **Observable test and decision:** In approximately 60 minutes, on a future bounded fictional task, freeze no more than eight classification cases plus positive and negative controls. A distinct reviewer reruns the preserved script where execution is already authorized and checks the same cases manually from raw records. Record mismatches and setup failures, then decide whether scripted aggregates may be used for that task class with this audit trail, or must remain unverified/model-reported. If execution capability is absent, perform only the artifact-freeze design and mark the test unrun.

- **Tradeoff:** Reproducible code can still encode a flawed rubric or omit relevant evidence; eight cases detect only local problems and say nothing about unaudited hidden behavior.

## N08 — Prospective effort and rework capture on one bounded task

- **Lens:** Decision-linked evidence; whether burden is measurable enough to support a later adoption or stop decision.
- **Proposal:** Before a future bounded task, agree a minimal event log for elapsed time, active work intervals, waiting, handoffs, retries/rework, and reviewer time. Capture only observable events with start/stop reason and owner; keep these operational burden measures separate from answer quality. At the end, test whether two people can reconstruct the same totals from the log without guessing.
- **Evidence hook:** The accepted review reports no actual active labor, token, queue, or cost measures; file counts and record growth are only maintenance proxies. This creates a basic evidence basis for a later decision without asserting efficiency or adding a large case set.
- **Observable test and decision:** In a future approximately 60-minute task window, collect the minimal log during one read-only fictional run, then spend a short closeout having a second person reconstruct elapsed/active/wait/rework totals. Record missing fields and disagreement, not a productivity score. Decide whether the logging burden is acceptable and which measures can be captured reliably before commissioning any comparative study. A single run cannot compare staffing options or establish ROI.
- **Prerequisites / unknowns:** A task and participants must be selected; native platform telemetry, active-time observability, and participant burden are unknown. Token/cost data are excluded unless already available without paid API or new infrastructure.
- **Tradeoff:** Logging interrupts work and may change behavior; elapsed time is not active labor, and one task is not representative. No historical effort can be reconstructed from file counts.
- **Old XID lineage:** X04-05/06 and the 2026-10-05 fictional rehearsal; this addresses missing measurement, not a claim that earlier runs were inefficient.
