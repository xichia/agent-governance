## Finance

**Observed contribution and response.** Finance produced an auditable integer-cent budget within the fictional AUD 6,000.00 ceiling, traced selected amounts to source cards, showed the 10% contingency arithmetic and headroom, described a lower-cost art-material alternative, and kept donated labor explicitly non-cash and unconfirmed. V1 totals AUD 5,665.00 (`deliverable.subtotal_cents=515000` cents = AUD 5,150.00; `contingency_cents=51500` cents = AUD 515.00; `total_cents=566500` cents = AUD 5,665.00); v2 removes the courtyard card and reports AUD 5,335.00 (`485000 + 48500` cents = AUD 4,850.00 + AUD 485.00). The source-priced 80-person workshop option and its tradeoff are explicit. Compare [archived v1 finance](../../../runs/SCI-20261005-01/archive/v1/finance.json) and [v2 finance](../../../runs/SCI-20261005-01/outputs/finance.json), especially `$.deliverable.budget`, `$.deliverable.calculation`, `$.deliverable.alternatives`, and `$.handoff`.

The first finance delivery failed before producing an artifact with “Selected model is at capacity”; the authorized same-session retry then yielded the role output. This is a reported delivery/startup capacity failure at the service boundary, not evidence of faulty finance reasoning; the run record does not independently diagnose the service cause. The successful v1/v2 budget response removed the courtyard spend, preserved the ceiling and acknowledged the change. Finance also handed off relevant assumptions and open questions on inventory, staffing and layout. No unresolved arithmetic defect was identified in the recorded independent recomputation; real supplier prices, volunteer availability and layout remain outside its evidence.

**Priority recommendation — P1, coordinator/service function with finance handoff.** Keep startup-capacity failures distinct from finance deliverable defects, and make a blocked/no-artifact state explicit until a successful delivery. Benefit: avoids misattribution and prevents downstream work from treating a missing budget as a finance decision. Tradeoff: retry/reassignment policy consumes run time and must stay within the owner-approved session limits. Next-run handling test: use an explicitly labeled mock-dispatcher fixture that returns a startup-capacity failure before producing an artifact, then a successful same-session retry. Confirm the failed attempt yields no accepted finance artifact, the retry is separately logged, and risk cannot start before a matching-version budget handoff. This tests handling only; it does not induce or establish live service-capacity behavior. For finance itself, retain the concise arithmetic/assumption/alternative envelope and test totals independently in v1 and v2.

## Creative / programme

**Observed contribution and response.** Creative designed a coherent plain-language science-and-art programme with setup and pack-down in the event window, a continuing quiet-space option, source-linked activity rows, and staffing/resource assumptions. V1 exposed two consequential design weaknesses rather than hiding them: the host did not span the quiet option/public interval, and the proposed 40-chair room transfers placed stewards in concurrent crowd-direction and furniture tasks. Risk recorded these as `RSK-01`–`RSK-03` and the change log as `CON-001`/`CON-002`; see [archived v1 creative](../../../runs/SCI-20261005-01/archive/v1/creative.json) (`$.deliverable.programme`, `$.deliverable.staff_assignments`, `$.deliverable.unresolved_questions`) and [change log](../../../runs/SCI-20261005-01/change-log.json) (`$.conflicts`).

In v2, creative removed courtyard activity, kept 80 attendees within Hall/Quiet capacities, allocated chairs 60/20 without transfers, set the quiet host to the 18:30–20:30 public interval, and preserved setup/pack-down. This is a useful recovery under `CH-001`, recorded in [v2 creative](../../../runs/SCI-20261005-01/outputs/creative.json) (`$.deliverable.programme`, `$.deliverable.staff_assignments`, `$.handoff.change_ack_ids`). It appropriately left floor-plan, route and volunteer availability open. A material issue remains: the proposal allows Quiet Room attendees to rejoin Hall refreshments although seating is planned 60 Hall/20 Quiet; risk identifies that the Hall seating case may then exceed the planned 60 seats. The reviewer retained this as an explicit human decision, not a cleared plan.

**Priority recommendation — P1, creative with risk review.** Model room attendance and seating during transitions and refreshments explicitly, including the “quiet-room attendees rejoin” case; keep it blocked/open if the brief lacks a feasible seating decision. Benefit: catches a practical inconsistency before integration. Tradeoff: may require changing the activity or asking the owner for a seating/service choice, adding coordination time. Next-run test: for every public interval, reconcile room attendance to allocated seats and resource capacity; reviewer checks the refreshment case against the final programme and risk register, with any unsupported arrangement left as an open decision.

## Risk

**Observed contribution and response.** Risk raised useful, scenario-specific matters: crowd flow and chair movement, step-free layout and competing steward duties, quiet-host coverage, food/allergen/service unknowns, and privacy boundaries. Its v1 issues were sufficiently concrete to drive schedule-level corrections and the run’s human-decision list; it did not present proposed mitigations as implemented. See [archived v1 risk](../../../runs/SCI-20261005-01/archive/v1/risk.json) (`$.deliverable.risks[]`, IDs `RSK-01`–`RSK-05`) and [v2 risk](../../../runs/SCI-20261005-01/outputs/risk.json) (same paths/IDs). V2 rechecked the indoor plan, accurately distinguished schedule-level fixes from unverified real-world layout/staffing, and documented unresolved seating, food, accessibility and privacy decisions. It acknowledged finance/creative inputs and `CH-001` in its handoff.

One initial schema-alignment correction moved risk-card citations into `source_ids` and retained venue/resource/programme support in `supporting_source_ids`; this was a citation-category mismatch, not evidence that the substantive risks were poor. The final structure makes primary risk provenance and supporting scenario facts distinguishable. A remaining material risk is the Hall refreshment/seating case (`RSK-01`), appropriately still open; review also leaves actual layout and staffing unresolved. See [metrics](../../../runs/SCI-20261005-01/metrics.md) for the correction record and [interface](../../../planning/events-agency/rehearsal/interface.md), which specifies risk-card references for risk rows but does not fully spell out this two-field citation convention.

**Priority recommendation — P1, risk contract/interface owner with risk function.** State the primary-versus-supporting source convention in the interface and use it consistently in risk rows. Benefit: reduces avoidable schema repair while preserving traceability to underlying venue/resource evidence. Tradeoff: a more explicit schema adds fields and review burden. Next-run test: a risk row cites at least one applicable risk card in `source_ids`; supporting factual evidence is retained separately; the checker accepts the row and the reviewer can trace both kinds without losing substantive issue text. Separately, retain a next-run check for unresolved occupancy/seating cases rather than closing them as mitigated.

## Marketing

**Observed contribution and response.** Marketing created clear, welcoming, draft-only copy, linked its programme IDs in order and room IDs to the used rooms, acknowledged all upstream dependencies, and made the v2 copy remove courtyard references while preserving human-review limits. V1 copy clearly marked itself fictional and not an invitation/ticket offer; v2 copy also describes the proposed two-hour programme and leaves access, staffing and food unconfirmed. Compare [archived v1 marketing](../../../runs/SCI-20261005-01/archive/v1/marketing.json) and [v2 marketing](../../../runs/SCI-20261005-01/outputs/marketing.json), `$.deliverable.marketing` and `$.handoff`.

The initial schema batch also corrected title/audience literal prose. Attribution needs care: the interface requires matching `title` and `attendance` fields and says the checker validates references, but the checker additionally requires the draft prose to contain the title and state “80 adults” (`check.py`, marketing checks); this prose rule is not stated plainly in the written interface. Thus the correction records a real acceptance mismatch, but the evidence does not justify treating the whole mismatch as individual marketing carelessness. The final v1/v2 handoffs satisfy it. No publication or factual claims were made beyond the fictional draft.

**Priority recommendation — P1, marketing with interface/checker owner.** Make prose acceptance explicit and testable in the interface; marketing should include exact event title and an unambiguous audience statement in the copy. Benefit: reduces schema-repair churn and keeps copy aligned with checker expectations. Tradeoff: literal checks can reject equally truthful paraphrases and may constrain editorial voice. Next-run test: before integration, checker and interface examples agree on exact prose requirements; marketing v1 and v2 each pass the title/audience wording check, preserve draft-only status, and contain no v2 courtyard claim.
