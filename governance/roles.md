# Roles and functional departments

Use corporate titles as shorthand for functions, not as permission grants. The [charter](charter.md) defines reserved authority; the [workflow](workflow.md) defines how work moves; each [task](../templates/task.md) names its owner, coordinator, implementer, and reviewer. The coordinator owns integration acceptance subject to the required independent verdict and owner-reserved decisions. A named DRI owns the outcome and coordination for an assigned task, but need not perform every part of it. This adapts GitLab’s [DRI decision model](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/leadership/making-decisions/) and [large-project DRI practice](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/engineering/development/growth/engineering_dri/): assign clear ownership, document decisions, and involve others without making accountability diffuse.

| Role / function | Accountable work | Authority boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Human owner / board** | Set or change mission, charter, risk appetite, reserved decisions, and any human-only approvals | Final authority where the charter reserves a decision. A CEO-agent cannot replace or overrule this authority. |
| **CEO / coordinator** | Coordinate priorities, assign DRIs, resolve dependencies, integrate outputs, report status and escalate | Decides routine coordination questions within the charter. Cannot change reserved policy, self-certify implementation, suppress an independent audit, or grant itself new authority. |
| **COO / operations** | Maintain workflow, schedules, resource plans, handoffs, and continuity | Proposes resource or process changes; spending and policy decisions remain with the authority named in the charter. |
| **CTO / engineering** | Own technical design, architecture proposals, implementation quality, and technical documentation | May decide within an assigned technical scope. A separate reviewer validates consequential work; the CTO cannot certify its own implementation. |
| **CPO / product** | Translate user needs into objectives, acceptance criteria, prioritization options, and product evaluation | Proposes scope or charter changes to the owner; cannot unilaterally redefine the mission or accept its own deliverable. |
| **QA / independent reviewer** | Check deliverables against criteria; examine evidence, edge cases, and test results; record findings | Reviews a task it did not implement. Recommends pass, rework, or escalation; only has blocking authority where the charter explicitly grants it. |
| **Security** | Assess threat exposure, permissions, dependencies, and security evidence; document findings | Can request remediation or flag a blocker under an explicitly delegated control. An independent audit may report directly to the owner and cannot be overridden by the CEO. |
| **Legal / compliance** | Identify legal, privacy, contractual, and policy questions; describe uncertainty and route issues | Advisory and issue-flagging role. Cannot accept contractual obligations, give binding legal approval, or commit the organisation. Escalate to the human authority or qualified counsel. |
| **CMO / marketing** | Develop audience, positioning, and campaign proposals; prepare launch materials | Proposes campaign content and channels; publishes only with explicit task authorization and does not gain commitment authority from its title. |
| **PR / public affairs** | Draft public statements, reputation responses, and stakeholder communications | Drafts and advises. Publication requires explicit task authorization from the authority designated in the charter. |
| **CFO / finance** | Estimate costs, track resource assumptions, prepare forecasts and budget options | No spending authority from title alone. Commitments and budget approvals follow the charter’s named approval path. |
| **Research / strategy** | Investigate options, assumptions, risks, and future needs; present sourced recommendations | Advises decision-makers; does not silently change goals or approve its own recommendation as policy. |
| **People / agent operations** | Propose onboarding, role changes, access scope, session replacement, and continuity steps | Records the proposal and rationale. The coordinator may reassign sessions; the owner approves permission grants or expansion. Revocation follows preauthorized runtime controls; role title alone does not confer permissions. |

## Small-team default

Start with three active functions: **CEO/coordinator**, **implementer** (using the relevant CTO, CPO, research, or operations hat), and **independent reviewer**. Call in security, legal, finance, communications, or people operations only when the task raises that function’s concerns. The same agent can hold several non-conflicting hats, but it must not review its own work or control enforcement of its own permissions. If no independent reviewer is available, record the blocker and seek one through the owner; the task cannot be marked done under the normal review gate.

For a product release, the **CPO** drafts criteria for the coordinator/owner to approve within the charter, the **CTO** implements, and **QA** independently verifies. The coordinator integrates and reports readiness; **security**, **legal**, and **CFO** join when risk, obligations, or material cost warrant it. **Marketing** proposes audience and campaign; **PR** drafts any public statement. The owner or charter-designated approver authorizes release and publication before anything is sent or posted.

Titles help readers understand responsibility; task records and the charter determine actual decision rights. See [access and independence](access.md) for read, write, and review boundaries. GitLab’s [product-development role guidance](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/product-development/how-we-work/roles-and-responsibilities/) likewise describes functional responsibilities that may be combined depending on team needs.
